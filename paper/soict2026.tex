\begin{abstract}
Archive ordering and proxy window labels can distort campus log anomaly evaluation. On selective HUFLIT extracts, we compare (A)~an archive-order stream-position protocol with validation contamination and (B)~a $1{,}000{,}000$-line exact-deduplicated, source-bounded, backup-day-held-out protocol, using a custom similarity parser and HTTP/keyword \emph{proxy} labels. Under Protocol~A, test-window prevalence reaches $80.5\%$ because a window is positive if any of $L{=}20$ lines is positive; an always-anomaly classifier then obtains F1$\approx0.892$, while a masked TCN--Transformer under $\tau=\mu{+}3\sigma$ scores only $0.0395\pm0.0163$ F1 despite ROC-AUC $0.7226\pm0.0178$ (AUPRC lift over prevalence $\approx{+}0.031$). Under Protocol~B (train 15--17~Jun, val 18~Jun, test 19~Jun 2026 backup days), test prevalence falls to $56.4\%$ (always-anomaly F1 baseline $0.721$); a DeepLog-style LSTM retains the strongest mean AUPRC lift ($+0.138$) but with large seed variance, masked models collapse near chance, and PCA stays more stable ($+0.098$). Keyword/status template ablation only partially reduces PCA lift ($+0.098\rightarrow+0.084$). Threshold comparison shows that $\mu{+}3\sigma$ suppresses recall, whereas validation-selected thresholds raise F1 toward the high-prevalence trivial baseline. Under Protocol~B, the TCN increases batch-1 PLL latency from $18.5$ to $51.1$~ms without a consistent accuracy benefit.
\keywords{AIOps \and Log Anomaly Detection \and Proxy Labels \and Experimental Validity \and TCN--Transformer \and Campus Infrastructure.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Campus IT services emit heterogeneous operational logs. Sequence detectors can score unusual template patterns~\cite{ref_deeplog,ref_loganomaly,ref_logrobust,ref_swisslog}, but archive packaging, duplicate backups, and heuristic labels can invent ``temporal'' structure that models then appear to learn. Prior empirical studies already show that training-data selection, grouping, class distribution, and noise strongly affect public LAD benchmarks~\cite{ref_le_zhang_2022,ref_albert_2025}. We extend that validity agenda to a setting those studies do not isolate: institutional \emph{backup archives} whose folder order is not event time, with exact raw-line contamination audits, OR-window prevalence inflation, proxy-label/feature coupling, and negative evidence for a masked TCN--Transformer.

This paper treats those threats as first-class experimental objects. We study a masked TCN--Transformer and a no-TCN ablated Transformer on HUFLIT selective extracts under two protocols: (A)~an archive-order stream-position split with validation contamination; (B)~a jointly decontaminated protocol (exact-line deduplication, boundary-safe windows, and backup-day holdout). We add trivial baselines (always-anomaly; no-skill AUPRC $=$ prevalence), report AUPRC lift over prevalence within each protocol, and compare fixed versus validation-selected thresholds. We do \emph{not} claim an LLM root-cause system.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Validity protocol on campus archives}: measure OR-window prevalence inflation against an always-anomaly trivial baseline, audit exact raw-line and template-window contamination, and retain dual-protocol artifacts (\texttt{huflit\_baselines.json}, \texttt{huflit\_v2\_*}).
    \item \textbf{Joint decontamination protocol}: Protocol~B applies exact-line SHA-1 dedup, windows that never cross (backup-day, source, member), and backup-day holdout; DeepLog-style and LogAnomaly-lite baselines are evaluated on the same arrays. We do \emph{not} claim a causal decomposition of the three factors.
    \item \textbf{Threshold-aware reading}: fixed $\mu{+}3\sigma$ versus validation-selected F1 thresholds with precision/recall, separating ranking from calibration.
    \item \textbf{Negative TCN evidence}: on both protocols the TCN does not consistently beat a masked Transformer without TCN, while increasing leave-one-out scoring latency; keyword/status ablation shows only partial proxy-token leakage into PCA lift.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Log Parsing and Template Extraction}
Log parsing transforms free-form messages into event templates by separating constants from dynamic parameters~\cite{ref_drain3,ref_spell}. Drain uses a fixed-depth parse tree~\cite{ref_drain3}; Spell uses streaming sequence matching~\cite{ref_spell}. Our artifact does \emph{not} implement Drain or Drain3. It applies regex masking and a linear scan over equal-length templates, merging tokens when positional similarity exceeds $0.5$. We call this the \emph{custom similarity parser} and avoid attributing Drain3 properties or throughput to it.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Log Anomaly Detection}
Sequence-based anomaly detection models learn temporal transition dynamics over sequences of log templates~\cite{ref_deeplog,ref_loganomaly,ref_pca}. Du et al.~\cite{ref_deeplog} introduced DeepLog (LSTM next-token prediction). Meng et al.~\cite{ref_loganomaly} proposed LogAnomaly with semantic template vectors. SwissLog~\cite{ref_swisslog} and LogRobust~\cite{ref_logrobust} address instability and interleaved threads. Classical reconstruction baselines such as PCA on count vectors remain competitive on some structured logs~\cite{ref_pca}. Recent Transformer--TCN hybrids~\cite{ref_tranconvad}, multi-scale semantic models~\cite{ref_logmuse_2026}, and contrastive masked objectives~\cite{ref_contralog_2026} continue this line. Public corpora such as Loghub~\cite{ref_loghub} remain the standard sanity setting; we defer a full HDFS/BGL re-run under the present harness to future work.

\subsection{Experimental Validity in Log Anomaly Detection}
Le and Zhang~\cite{ref_le_zhang_2022} show that training-data selection, grouping, class distribution, data noise, and early-detection settings can overturn optimistic LAD conclusions on public benchmarks. Albert~\cite{ref_albert_2025} argues that training should precede testing chronologically and analyzes grouping, anomaly ratio, and duplicate sequences. Our contribution is complementary rather than redundant: we study an institutional campus \emph{backup archive} where packaging order is not wall-clock event time; we report exact raw-line hash contamination and OR-window prevalence inflation with an always-anomaly baseline; we probe proxy-label/feature coupling via template ablation; and we provide negative evidence for a masked TCN--Transformer under that archive protocol.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{System Architecture Overview}
The measured system has two stages:
(i) \textbf{Parsing}: selected syslog messages are tokenized by the custom similarity parser;
(ii) \textbf{Masked sequence scoring}: sliding template windows are corrupted by true token replacement, encoded by an optional causal TCN plus Transformer, and scored by leave-one-position-out pseudo-log-likelihood (PLL).

\subsection{Masked TCN-Transformer Denoising Sequence Model}
We formulate the detector as a masked template language model: 15\% of input tokens are replaced by a reserved mask token, representations pass through a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN)~\cite{ref_tcn} and Transformer self-attention~\cite{ref_attention}, and a linear vocabulary head predicts the original identities only at selected positions.

\subsubsection{Embedding and Dynamic Masking}
A sliding window of length $L = 20$ with stride $S = 10$ extracts template sequences $\mathbf{X} = [T_1, T_2, \ldots, T_L]$. During training, 15\% of tokens are replaced with \texttt{[MASK]} ($ID = |\mathcal{V}|+1$), distinct from padding ($ID = 0$). Padding masks are applied in self-attention. Embeddings use sinusoidal positional encodings~\cite{ref_attention}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{E} = \text{Embedding}(\mathbf{X}) + \mathbf{P}_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_{\text{model}}}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) Layer}
Dilated causal convolutions~\cite{ref_tcn} with $d \in \{1, 2\}$ extract local patterns. The TCN branch uses causal convolutions; the subsequent Transformer encoder remains bidirectional, as intended by the masked-PLL objective:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{y}(t) = (\mathbf{x} *_d f)(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} f(i) \cdot \mathbf{x}(t - d \cdot i)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{H}_{\text{TCN}} = \text{LayerNorm}(\text{Proj}(\mathbf{E}) + \text{GELU}(\text{CausalTCN}(\mathbf{E})))
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Transformer Encoder and Scoring}
A single Transformer encoder layer (pre-LN, multi-head attention, GELU FFN) contextualizes the sequence. Training minimizes masked cross-entropy. Let $\mathcal{V}$ be the set of non-padding positions and
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{X}^{(-t)}=[T_1,\ldots,\texttt{[MASK]}_t,\ldots,T_L].
\end{equation}
Inference uses leave-one-position-out PLL:
\begin{equation}
    S(\mathbf{X}) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{V}|} \sum_{t\in\mathcal{V}} -\log P(T_t \mid \mathbf{X}^{(-t)}).
\end{equation}
The primary threshold is $\tau=\mu_{\mathrm{val}}+3\sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$ on nominal validation scores; as a diagnostic we also report a validation-selected F1 threshold applied to test scores.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\subsection{Corpus and Window Labels}
We stream selective June daily backup archives from a local institutional campus extract, focusing on application/service syslog members from aca, careerhub, and courses. We skip \texttt{fortigate.csv} (heterogeneous firewall CSV schema; not comparable to the syslog template stream without a separate parser) and members ${>}500$~MiB (computational bound for the selective ingest). Protocol~B keeps the first $1$M unique raw lines under per-day and per-file caps ($200$k lines/day; $40$k/file). Proxy line labels use HTTP $4$xx/$5$xx, deny/block fields, and keywords---not SOC adjudication. A window is positive iff \emph{any} of its $L$ lines is positive (OR aggregation). Under independence, $1-(1-p)^{L}$ with $p{=}0.0796$ predicts prevalence $\approx0.81$, matching Protocol~A's $80.5\%$ test rate.

\subsection{Protocol A (archive-order split with validation contamination)}
Records are ordered by backup day $\rightarrow$ ZIP $\rightarrow$ member $\rightarrow$ in-member position, then split $80$/$10$/$10$ before windowing. This is \emph{not} event time: windows may cross file/service boundaries. A raw-line hash audit found $36.235\%$ of unique validation lines also in training and $11.382\%$ validation/test line overlap; train/test exact raw-line overlap is only $0.0176\%$ of unique test lines ($13$ shared hashes), so Protocol~A's primary risk is archive-order structure and \emph{validation contamination} (validation is used for threshold calibration) rather than massive train$\rightarrow$test line leakage. Exact template-window overlap is likewise small on test ($13$ of $7{,}573$ unique test windows). Results: \texttt{huflit\_baselines.json}.

\subsection{Protocol B (dedup + source-bounded + backup-day holdout)}
Protocol~B \emph{jointly} applies exact-line deduplication, boundary-safe windowing, and backup-day holdout; we therefore treat A$\rightarrow$B as a comparison of protocols, not a causal ablation of archive ordering alone. We keep the first SHA-1 of each \emph{raw line} ($262{,}194$ duplicates skipped while filling $1$M unique lines), emit windows only inside one (backup-day, source, member) group, and assign backup days $15$--$17$~Jun$\rightarrow$train, $18$~Jun$\rightarrow$val, $19$~Jun$\rightarrow$test. Day folders are backup collection periods, not verified per-event timestamps. Test-window prevalence is $56.36\%$ (always-anomaly F1 baseline $0.721$).

Global exact-line deduplication may also remove legitimate repeated events that recur identically across backup days; we therefore treat it as a \emph{conservative decontamination} step that can alter the backup-day-held-out test distribution, and report this as an additional validity limitation rather than a claim that every duplicate is a backup artifact.

Raw-line dedup does \emph{not} imply unique template windows. Exact template-ID window sequence overlap (SHA-1 of the integer window vector): $505$ of $11{,}065$ distinct validation windows ($4.56\%$) also occur in training, and $110$ of $11{,}137$ distinct test windows ($0.99\%$) also occur in training (\texttt{huflit\_v2\_split\_meta.json}).

\subsection{Results}
On Protocol~A, the DeepLog-style LSTM leads ROC-AUC ($0.781$); LogAnomaly-lite is close ($0.777$). The TCN is \emph{worse} than no-TCN on mean F1/AUC and much slower at batch-1 PLL. The always-anomaly F1 baseline ($0.892$) exceeds every learned F1 at $\mu{+}3\sigma$; AUPRC must be read against prevalence $0.805$.

On Protocol~B (Table~\ref{tab_v2}), DeepLog-style and LogAnomaly-lite retain the strongest mean AUPRC lifts ($+0.138$ and $+0.112$), but with large seed variance (DeepLog-style ROC-AUC $0.657\pm0.134$). Masked models lose backup-day ranking (AUC$\approx0.49$--$0.53$). PCA keeps a smaller but more stable lift ($+0.098$). Table~\ref{tab_thr}: $\mu{+}3\sigma$ starves recall for most detectors (PCA/DeepLog-style/masked Transformer recall $\approx0$--$0.01$), while thresholds selected on validation and evaluated on \emph{test} recover F1$\approx0.73$--$0.75$---near the always-anomaly test baseline ($0.721$)---showing that much of the apparent F1 gain is driven by prevalence and threshold calibration rather than strong ranking quality. Under Protocol~B, TCN remains slower ($51$ vs.\ $19$~ms) without consistent accuracy gains.

Table~\ref{tab_ablation}: remapping $167$ proxy-trigger templates ($\approx9.3\%$ of test tokens) to padding reduces PCA lift from $+0.098$ to $+0.084$ without collapsing ranking to chance, while the masked Transformer stays near chance before and after ablation. For the masked models, remapping to padding removes these positions from attention and from the PLL average rather than introducing a dedicated ablation token; for PCA, pad IDs are simply omitted from the count vector. Residual correlation remains after template-level trigger ablation; without SOC labels, it cannot be interpreted as validated operational anomaly signal.

Due to privacy restrictions, raw campus telemetry cannot be redistributed. We retain code, configuration, aggregate split statistics, and derived evaluation JSON artifacts in the project repository for co-author reproduction; a public mirror is contingent on institutional approval.

\section{Discussion \& Threats to Validity}
\textbf{What sequence means}: Protocol~A orders archive members, not wall-clock events. Protocol~B jointly removes cross-member windows and duplicate raw lines but still uses backup-day folders rather than verified timestamps. Seed variance does not capture day-to-day sampling uncertainty because Protocol~B contains a single held-out backup day.

\textbf{Window OR labels}: OR aggregation manufactures high prevalence and makes the always-anomaly F1 baseline a necessary reference (not an upper bound: a perfect detector still has F1$=1$). Ranking metrics and lift-over-prevalence must accompany thresholded F1; raw AUPRC lift is interpreted within each protocol and not directly compared across prevalences.

\textbf{Proxy labels and label--feature coupling}: HTTP status tokens, deny/block fields, and keyword heuristics both \emph{define} proxy labels and often remain visible in template text. Proxy labels also select nominal-only training windows and the nominal validation scores used for $\mu{+}3\sigma$. Models may therefore learn the labeling rule rather than operational anomalies. Our keyword/status template ablation (Table~\ref{tab_ablation}) supports partial leakage without claiming residual operational signal. Gold SOC labels remain required before operational claims.

\textbf{Parser}: The custom parser has no gold template annotations; the $0.5$ merge threshold is untested for sensitivity, and parser merge errors may alter event frequency and sequence structure~\cite{ref_le_zhang_2022}.

\textbf{Corpus selection}: Skipping FortiGate CSV and ${>}500$~MiB members is a deliberate selective-ingest policy (schema mismatch and compute bound), not a claim of full-archive coverage.

\textbf{Ethics}: Campus telemetry stays private; any future release needs redaction and authorization.

\section{Conclusion}
We showed that OR window labels can inflate prevalence and make thresholded F1 look stronger than ranking warrants on HUFLIT campus logs, and that a jointly decontaminated backup-day-held-out protocol changes conclusions relative to an archive-ordered stream split. Masked TCN/Transformer ranking collapses under Protocol~B while PCA and classical sequence baselines retain unstable or modest AUPRC lifts; TCN adds latency without a reliable benefit. Keyword/status template ablation only partially reduces PCA lift. Future work: factorized protocol ablations, additional backup-day folds, event timestamps, SOC labels, and Loghub~\cite{ref_loghub} sanity checks under the same harness.
