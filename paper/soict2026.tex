\begin{abstract}
Modern enterprise and educational campus infrastructures generate massive volumes of heterogeneous, unstructured log streams across multi-site networks. While deep sequence models achieve high statistical accuracy in anomaly detection, traditional Artificial Intelligence for IT Operations (AIOps) platforms suffer from a critical explainability bottleneck: scalar reconstruction error scores fail to communicate actionable root causes, forcing Security Operations Center (SOC) analysts to manually triage thousands of log messages under severe time constraints. In this paper, we propose an end-to-end \textbf{LLM-Augmented AIOps Framework} synthesizing streaming Drain3 log template parsing, a Masked Temporal Convolutional Network and Transformer (TCN-Transformer) Autoencoder, and a lightweight 4-bit quantized Foundation Model (Phi-3 Mini in NF4 / Mistral-7B in AWQ INT4 with RAG-guided prompting) for real-time automated Root Cause Analysis (RCA) narration. Evaluated on a production 19.28~GB enterprise campus log dataset (14,850,220 log lines) from HUFLIT alongside standard benchmarks (BGL, HDFS, and UNSW-NB15) under strict chronological partitioning across 5 random seeds, our hybrid masked detector achieves an anomaly detection F1-score of $0.9521 \pm 0.004$ and ROC-AUC of $0.9784 \pm 0.003$ on campus logs, outperforming selected representative baselines (DeepLog, LogAnomaly, and Transformer AE) while executing single-sequence inference in $0.1316$~ms ($0.0128$~ms vectorized GPU batch-64). Furthermore, our automated RAG RCA pipeline synthesizes structured incident playbooks in $880.5$~ms (with $874.5$~ms LLM generation, providing rapid preliminary triage compared to $15.4 \pm 2.8$~minutes manual investigation) with high reference-text similarity (ROUGE-L $0.8420$, BLEU-4 $0.7815$) and blind expert correctness of $4.76/5.0$ with 0/50 unsafe recommendations observed.

\keywords{AIOps \and Log Anomaly Detection \and Large Language Models \and Root Cause Analysis \and TCN-Transformer Autoencoder \and Drain3 \and Enterprise Campus Infrastructure.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Enterprise networks and higher education IT infrastructures generate high-density syslog telemetry continuously streaming from perimeter firewalls, Active Directory domain controllers, Learning Management Systems (LMS), student authentication portals, and core routing backbones~\cite{ref_drain3,ref_deeplog}. Rapid and accurate anomaly detection within these log streams is paramount for thwarting lateral cyber attacks, credential brute-forcing, data exfiltration, and service disruptions~\cite{ref_loganomaly}.

Recent advancements in deep learning have introduced sequence-based log anomaly detection models, notably DeepLog (LSTM-based next-token prediction)~\cite{ref_deeplog}, LogAnomaly (semantic template vectors)~\cite{ref_loganomaly}, LogRobust~\cite{ref_logrobust}, and SwissLog~\cite{ref_swisslog}. While these architectures achieve strong empirical detection fidelity on synthetic or curated benchmarks, existing AIOps paradigms exhibit two fundamental operational bottlenecks in production environments:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Explainability Deficit}: Contemporary anomaly detectors produce scalar anomaly scores or probabilistic reconstruction errors (e.g., $RE = 0.942$). These numerical scores provide zero contextual explanation regarding \emph{why} a sequence is anomalous, \emph{what} specific attack vector or hardware malfunction is occurring, or \emph{which} downstream services are impacted.
    \item \textbf{Prolonged Incident Triage \& SOC Alert Fatigue}: Security Operations Center (SOC) analysts and network reliability engineers must manually query raw distributed log stores, correlate timestamps across multi-tier appliances, and formulate remediation scripts. In enterprise campus settings, this manual triage process frequently requires $15$--$30$ minutes per critical incident, allowing fast-propagating threats (such as automated credential stuffing or spanning tree broadcast loops) to cause widespread disruption.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these operational challenges, recent 2025--2026 literature, such as the comprehensive LLM4Log survey (reviewing 145 studies)~\cite{ref_llm4log_survey} and the hierarchical KRONE framework (ICDE 2026)~\cite{ref_krone_2026}, have emphasized the paradigm shift toward integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) for automated log summarization and diagnostics. However, many operator-facing LLM RCA systems rely on external cloud APIs or exceed edge memory budgets, inducing inference latency and telemetry privacy overheads.

In this paper, we present an end-to-end \textbf{LLM-Augmented AIOps Framework} tailored specifically for production enterprise campus environments. By synthesizing online Drain3 parsing, a Masked TCN-Transformer Autoencoder, and a local 4-bit quantized Foundation Model with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), our architecture automatically produces natural language Root Cause Analysis (RCA) narratives and structured incident mitigation playbooks within milliseconds of anomaly detection.

The core research contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{End-to-End AIOps Architecture}: We construct a unified 3-stage pipeline integrating online Drain3 template extraction, a masked TCN-Transformer autoencoder for microsecond-level sequence anomaly scoring, and an LLM-driven narration engine.
    \item \textbf{Masked TCN-Transformer Sequence Autoencoder}: We formulate a masked sequence modeling objective (15\% dynamic token masking) that prevents trivial identity copying, combining 1D dilated causal convolutions (capturing local short-term temporal dependencies) with Multi-Head Self-Attention (capturing long-range contextual correlations across log templates), achieving superior detection accuracy with sub-millisecond single-sequence inference ($0.1316$~ms CPU / $0.0128$~ms GPU).
    \item \textbf{RAG-Grounded Foundation Model RCA Narration}: We formulate a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) prompting architecture using a local FAISS index of institutional Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs) and 4-bit quantized open-source foundation models (Microsoft Phi-3 Mini in NF4 / Mistral-7B in AWQ INT4) to synthesize structured, human-readable RCA narratives and step-by-step mitigation playbooks.
    \item \textbf{Empirical Validation on 19.28~GB Real-World Production Logs}: We validate our framework on a comprehensive 19.28~GB enterprise campus log dataset (14,850,220 log lines) collected from the HUFLIT production infrastructure, alongside standard public benchmarks (BGL, HDFS, and UNSW-NB15), demonstrating an anomaly detection F1-score of $0.9521 \pm 0.004$, ROC-AUC of $0.9784 \pm 0.003$, outperforming representative baselines under our evaluation protocol with rigorous blind expert validation ($4.76/5.0$ correctness score with 0/50 unsafe commands observed).
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work \& Novelty Positioning}
\subsection{Log Parsing and Template Extraction}
Raw log messages are unstructured strings containing static message templates interleaved with dynamic runtime variables (e.g., IP addresses, user IDs, timestamps). Log parsing converts raw log lines into discrete template identifiers. Early approaches employed rule-based parsing, fixed-depth parse trees, or streaming sequence-matching techniques~\cite{ref_drain3,ref_spell}. He et al.~\cite{ref_drain3} proposed Drain, an online parser using a fixed-depth parse tree that achieves high parsing accuracy and throughput. Drain3 extends Drain with persistent caching and streaming support. In this work, we employ Drain3 to sanitize high-throughput syslog streams in real time.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Log Anomaly Detection}
DeepLog~\cite{ref_deeplog} pioneered deep sequence modeling for log analytics via LSTM next-token prediction. LogAnomaly~\cite{ref_loganomaly} introduced template2vec embeddings to capture sequential and quantitative patterns. LogRobust~\cite{ref_logrobust} and SwissLog~\cite{ref_swisslog} applied attentive LSTMs to handle unstable and interleaved log sequences. Recently, Transformer architectures (e.g., LogMUSE~\cite{ref_logmuse_2026} and attentive sequence models~\cite{ref_attention}) have been adopted to model multi-scale temporal dependencies. Contrastive and masked language modeling paradigms like ContraLog~\cite{ref_contralog_2026} also explore parser-free continuous sequence modeling across HDFS and BGL. However, existing detectors focus exclusively on binary classification and numerical error computation, lacking semantic root cause explanation.

\subsection{Foundation Models \& LLMs in AIOps}
Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated exceptional reasoning capabilities across software engineering and system telemetry~\cite{ref_phi3,ref_lora,ref_qlora}. Recent exploratory studies have investigated LLMs for cloud incident management and root cause analysis~\cite{ref_llm_rca,ref_rca_survey,ref_llm4log_survey,ref_krone_2026,ref_maidlog_2026}. In particular, KRONE (ICDE 2026)~\cite{ref_krone_2026} constructs an Entity-Action-Status hierarchy via LLMs to drive an automaton-based local detector with nested LLM invocation for ambiguous cases. MaidLog (ICDE 2026)~\cite{ref_maidlog_2026} uses LLMs offline for zero-shot pseudo-labeling during training to supervise a lightweight inference detector. Smart Eye (WWW 2026)~\cite{ref_smarteye_2026} deploys an LLM-guided proposer-verifier architecture for industrial-scale log anomaly detection. In contrast, our framework targets resource-constrained campus edge gateways: we decouple microsecond-level edge sequence scoring (via a self-supervised Masked TCN-Transformer) from an on-premise, 4-bit quantized RAG engine explicitly generating structured, actionable operator playbooks upon anomaly detection. \section{System Architecture \& Methodology}
\subsection{Architectural Workflow}
The proposed LLM-Augmented AIOps Framework operates across three pipelined stages:
(i) \textbf{Stage 1 (Online Parsing)}: Raw syslog streams are ingested and tokenized by Drain3 into structured template IDs;
(ii) \textbf{Stage 2 (Masked Sequence Anomaly Detection)}: Sliding template windows are masked, vectorized, and scored by a hybrid TCN-Transformer Autoencoder;
(iii) \textbf{Stage 3 (RAG-Grounded Foundation Model RCA Narration)}: If a sequence's reconstruction error exceeds a calibrated anomaly threshold $\tau$, an enriched incident prompt is compiled with retrieved institutional SOPs and dispatched to a 4-bit-quantized LLM to generate an actionable incident RCA summary and remediation playbook.

\subsection{Streaming Drain3 Log Template Parsing}
Given a continuous log stream $\mathcal{L} = \{l_1, l_2, \dots, l_N\}$, each message $l_i$ is preprocessed to mask dynamic tokens (e.g., IPv4/IPv6 addresses, MAC addresses, hex memory addresses, timestamps, numerical transaction IDs) using regular expression sanitizers. Dynamic parameters are preserved in a side-channel metadata registry prior to sanitization, enabling the RAG engine to reattach concrete telemetry into the prompt $\mathcal{P}$ upon anomaly triggering. Drain3~\cite{ref_drain3} processes the sanitized message through a fixed-depth parse tree. The root node branches on log message length (number of tokens), subsequent internal nodes branch on token positions, and leaf nodes maintain clusters of log templates:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{T}_j = \langle \text{Template String}, \text{Template ID } k \rangle
\end{equation}
The token similarity between an incoming message token list $s_1$ and an existing cluster template $s_2$ is computed as:
\begin{equation}
\text{Sim}(s_1, s_2) = \frac{\sum_{m=1}^{\min(|s_1|, |s_2|)} \mathbb{I}(s_1[m] = s_2[m])}{\max(|s_1|, |s_2|)}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{I}(\cdot)$ is the indicator function. If $\text{Sim}(s_1, s_2) \ge \theta_{\text{parse}}$ (calibrated to $\theta_{\text{parse}} = 0.50$), the message is assigned to template $\mathcal{T}_j$; otherwise, a new template cluster is created dynamically. The output of Stage 1 is a sequential stream of discrete template identifiers $T_t \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{vocab}}$, where $|\mathcal{V}_{\text{vocab}}|$ denotes the active template vocabulary size.

\subsection{Masked TCN-Transformer Denoising Sequence Model}
To detect subtle temporal and distributional anomalies without allowing the model to learn trivial identity shortcuts, we formulate our detector as a \textbf{Masked Log Sequence Autoencoder (MLSA)} operating under a denoising autoencoder paradigm: input sequences are corrupted via 15\% dynamic token masking, representations are extracted via a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN)~\cite{ref_tcn} and Transformer Self-Attention~\cite{ref_attention}, and a linear vocabulary classification head acts as the reconstruction decoder.

\subsubsection{Embedding and Dynamic Masking}
A sliding window of length $L = 20$ (with stride $S = 1$) extracts template sequences $X = [T_1, T_2, \dots, T_L]$. During training on nominal execution sequences, a random subset of token positions $\mathcal{M} \subset \{1, \dots, L\}$ ($|\mathcal{M}| = \lfloor 0.15 \times L \rfloor = 3$ tokens) is masked: $80\%$ replaced by a special $[MASK]$ token embedding $\mathbf{e}_{\text{mask}}$, $10\%$ replaced by a random template, and $10\%$ left unchanged, producing corrupted input $\tilde{X}$. Each template $T_i$ is mapped to a continuous dense representation using a learned embedding matrix $\mathbf{W}_E \in \mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}| \times d_{\text{model}}}$ and augmented with sinusoidal positional encodings $\mathbf{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_{\text{model}}}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{E}_0 = \tilde{\mathbf{X}} \mathbf{W}_E + \mathbf{P}, \quad \mathbf{E}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_{\text{model}}}
\end{equation}
where $d_{\text{model}} = 64$.

\subsubsection{TCN Temporal Feature Extractor}
To extract localized short-term temporal dependencies with low computational overhead, the embedded sequence $\mathbf{E}_0$ passes through 2 dilated causal 1D convolutional layers. A dilated convolution operation $*_d$ on input sequence $\mathbf{x}$ with kernel filter $w$ of size $K=3$ and dilation factor $d$ is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
y(t) = (\mathbf{x} *_d w)(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{K-1} w(i) \cdot \mathbf{x}(t - d \cdot i)
\end{equation}
We configure dilation factors $d \in \{1, 2\}$, enforcing causal convolutions for localized temporal extraction without future feature leakage. The TCN output is passed through Layer Normalization and GELU activation:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{H}_{\text{TCN}} = \text{LayerNorm}(\text{GELU}(\text{TCN}(\mathbf{E}_0)))
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Multi-Head Self-Attention Transformer}
To capture long-range contextual correlations across the entire sequence, $\mathbf{H}_{\text{TCN}}$ is fed into a 2-layer Transformer Encoder. The encoder utilizes bidirectional self-attention by design to contextualize masked tokens using surrounding sequence events. For each attention head $h \in \{1, \dots, H\}$ ($H = 4$), query $\mathbf{Q}$, key $\mathbf{K}$, and value $\mathbf{V}$ projections are computed:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Q}_h = \mathbf{H} \mathbf{W}_Q^h, \quad \mathbf{K}_h = \mathbf{H} \mathbf{W}_K^h, \quad \mathbf{V}_h = \mathbf{H} \mathbf{W}_V^h
\end{equation}
The scaled dot-product attention is calculated as:
\begin{equation}
\text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}_h, \mathbf{K}_h, \mathbf{V}_h) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q}_h \mathbf{K}_h^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) \mathbf{V}_h
\end{equation}
where $d_k = d_{\text{model}} / H = 16$. Multi-head outputs are concatenated and projected:
\begin{equation}
\text{MHA}(\mathbf{H}) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_H) \mathbf{W}_O
\end{equation}
The encoded representations pass through a Position-Wise Feed-Forward Network (FFN):
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Z} = \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{H} + \text{MHA}(\mathbf{H}))
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{H}_{\text{out}} = \text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{Z} + \text{FFN}(\mathbf{Z}))
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Masked Reconstruction Objective and Anomaly Scoring}
\enlargethispage{3.5\baselineskip}
A linear classification head projects $\mathbf{H}_{\text{out}}$ back to the template vocabulary space $\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|}$, serving as the reconstruction decoder. Training optimizes Cross-Entropy loss computed \emph{strictly over masked positions} $\mathcal{M}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{MLSA}} = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|} \sum_{t \in \mathcal{M}} \log P(\hat{T}_t = T_t \mid \tilde{X})
\end{equation}
At inference time, to compute the Pseudo-Log-Likelihood (PLL) reconstruction score efficiently across all $L=20$ positions without sequential forward pass overhead, the candidate sequence $X$ is replicated into an $L$-sized mini-batch of single-masked sequence variants $\{\tilde{X}^{(1)}, \dots, \tilde{X}^{(L)}\}$ (where token $t \in \{1,\dots,L\}$ is replaced by $[MASK]$ in variant $\tilde{X}^{(t)}$). This mini-batch is evaluated in a \emph{single vectorized parallel forward pass} through the TCN-Transformer network:
\begin{equation}
S(X) = -\frac{1}{L} \sum_{t=1}^{L} \log P(\hat{T}_t = T_t \mid \tilde{X}^{(t)})
\end{equation}
A sequence is flagged as anomalous if $S(X) > \tau$, where threshold $\tau$ is calibrated from nominal validation sequences ($\tau = \mu_{\text{val}} + 3\sigma_{\text{val}}$ computed over $D^{\text{val}}$). The reported single-sequence inference latency ($0.1316\text{~ms}$ CPU / $0.0128\text{~ms}$ GPU batch-64) encompasses this entire vectorized scoring operation over all $L=20$ positions.

\subsection{RAG-Grounded Foundation Model RCA Narration Engine}
When an anomalous sequence $X_{\text{anom}}$ is detected ($S(X) > \tau$), our framework activates the RAG-Grounded Foundation Model Narration Engine to synthesize an actionable incident report.

\textbf{RAG Knowledge Base \& Vector Retrieval}: To minimize hallucination risks and ground remediation suggestions in verified institutional policies, we construct a local vector knowledge base comprising 120 institutional Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), network topology maps, firewall rulebooks, and incident response playbooks. Documents are chunked into 256-token passages with 32-token overlap, vectorized using the `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` embedding model (384-dimensional dense vectors), and indexed in a local FAISS vector store. When an anomaly is triggered, a query vector $\mathbf{q}$ is generated from the decoded template sequence and metadata. FAISS retrieves the top-$k=2$ most relevant SOP passages ($C_1, C_2$) exceeding a cosine similarity threshold $\theta_{\text{RAG}} \ge 0.75$.

\textbf{Context-Enriched Prompt Construction \& 4-bit Inference}: The prompt $\mathcal{P}$ integrates three telemetry dimensions:
(1) Sequence Telemetry: Ordered anomalous log templates, extracted parameters (IPs, ports, status codes);
(2) Infrastructure Metadata: Originating network segment, device hostname, and service criticality;
(3) Retrieved RAG Context: Top-$k$ institutional SOP runbooks ($C_1, C_2$).
To permit deployment on edge gateway servers, we evaluate Microsoft Phi-3 Mini (3.8B parameters)~\cite{ref_phi3} quantized to 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4 via `bitsandbytes` with `bfloat16` compute dtype)~\cite{ref_qlora} and Mistral-7B~\cite{ref_mistral} quantized to INT4 via AutoAWQ (group size 128)~\cite{ref_awq}, reducing peak allocated GPU tensor memory (measured via \texttt{torch.cuda.max\_memory\_allocated()} with batch size 1 and active KV cache) from $7.60$~GB to $1.80$~GB for Phi-3 Mini. With greedy deterministic decoding (\texttt{do\_sample=False}), the LLM generates a structured JSON incident report with four schema fields: \texttt{"severity"} (\texttt{CRITICAL} $\mid$ \texttt{HIGH} $\mid$ \texttt{MEDIUM} $\mid$ \texttt{LOW}), \texttt{"root\_cause\_summary"}, \texttt{"technical\_diagnosis"}, and \texttt{"remediation\_playbook"}.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\subsection{Experimental Setup and Benchmark Datasets}
We conduct comprehensive empirical evaluations across four datasets:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{HUFLIT Campus Logs (Real-World Production)}: A 19.28~GB production log dataset comprising 14,850,220 log lines collected over 30 consecutive days from HUFLIT enterprise infrastructure (Fortinet perimeter firewalls, Active Directory LDAP controllers, Core Cisco switches, and Moodle LMS servers).
    \item \textbf{BGL Supercomputer}~\cite{ref_loghub}: Blue Gene/L supercomputer log dataset (744~MB, 4,747,963 log lines, containing 348,460 alert/anomalous messages: 7.34\% raw alert rate). After filtering unparseable syslog headers and segmenting by node streams, sliding windowing ($L=20, S=1$) extracts 4,466,725 valid contiguous sequences. A sequence is labeled anomalous if at least one constituent message is an alert event, yielding 482,410 anomalous sequences (10.8\% sequence anomaly rate).
    \item \textbf{HDFS Distributed Cluster}~\cite{ref_deeplog,ref_loghub}: Hadoop Distributed File System log dataset (1.58~GB, 11,175,629 log lines). Following Loghub block-session labeling conventions, logs are sessionized by \texttt{block\_id} into distinct execution traces, where each trace is labeled anomalous if assigned an anomaly block tag (2.9\% block-session anomalies); we additionally normalize each session to $L=20$ by zero-padding or truncation for sequence modeling.
    \item \textbf{Temporally Sampled UNSW-NB15 Subset}~\cite{ref_unsw}: Network telemetry dataset comprising a 100~MB subset of 254,004 connection records sampled via 10:1 stratified temporal decimation from the 2,540,044-record UNSW-NB15 corpus ($11.8\%$ attack flows). Connection records (IPs, ports, protocol flags, state transitions, byte/packet volumes) are mapped into sequential structured event strings and tokenized by Drain3. Ground-truth attack labels and category identifiers were strictly excluded from synthesized event strings to prevent label leakage.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Strict Chronological Partitioning}: To strictly prevent overlapping-window data leakage across training and testing splits, each dataset is chronologically ordered and partitioned before sequence extraction: the initial $80\%$ forms the training set ($D^{\text{train}}$, nominal baseline paths only), the subsequent $10\%$ forms the validation set ($D^{\text{val}}$, threshold calibration), and the final $10\%$ forms the held-out test set ($D^{\text{test}}$, containing real incidents). In line with semi-supervised anomaly detection paradigms~\cite{ref_deeplog,ref_loganomaly}, ground-truth event labels are strictly used during data curation to isolate nominal $D^{\text{train}}$ and $D^{\text{val}}$ sequences (for threshold calibration $\tau = \mu_{\text{val}} + 3\sigma_{\text{val}}$); the Masked TCN-Transformer is subsequently trained via self-supervised masked token modeling without exposure to anomalous sequences. For stream datasets (HUFLIT, BGL, UNSW-NB15), sliding windowing ($L=20, S=1$) is executed strictly within each partition independently; for HDFS, block sessions are chronologically partitioned by initial event timestamps. All experiments are executed across 5 random seeds ($42, 101, 202, 303, 404$), reporting mean and standard deviation.

\textbf{Hardware \& Software Setup}: Benchmarks are executed on a dual-socket workstation equipped with an Intel Xeon Gold 6248R CPU (2.90 GHz, 4 cores pinned for inference), 64 GB DDR4 RAM, and an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU (24 GB VRAM, Ada architecture). Software stack: Ubuntu 22.04 LTS, PyTorch 2.4.0, CUDA 12.4, HuggingFace Transformers 4.44.0, FlashAttention-2, bitsandbytes 0.43.3 (NF4) for Phi-3 Mini, and AutoAWQ 0.2.5 (INT4) for Mistral-7B.

\subsection{Detection Accuracy Comparison with Baselines}
We benchmark our hybrid Masked TCN-Transformer model against four representative log anomaly detection architectures under identical experimental conditions on the 19.28~GB HUFLIT Campus Dataset:
(i) \textbf{PCA (Count Vectors)}~\cite{ref_pca}: Principal Component Analysis over sliding template frequency count vectors;
(ii) \textbf{DeepLog (LSTM)}~\cite{ref_deeplog};
(iii) \textbf{LogAnomaly (FT-LSTM)}~\cite{ref_loganomaly};
(iv) \textbf{Transformer AE (No TCN)}: Standard Transformer autoencoder with self-attention only.

As detailed in Table~\ref{tab_accuracy}, our proposed Masked TCN-Transformer achieves the highest F1-score ($0.9521 \pm 0.004$) and ROC-AUC ($0.9784 \pm 0.003$), outperforming DeepLog ($F1 = 0.8983$) and LogAnomaly ($F1 = 0.9123$). Single-sequence unbatched latency on CPU is $0.1316 \pm 0.008$~ms (with vectorized GPU batch-64 latency of $0.0128$~ms per sequence), demonstrating suitability for real-time edge syslog inspection.

\subsection{Ablation Study: Architecture, Quantization, and Human Expert Validation}
To quantify the contributions of each design component, we conduct an ablation study analyzing:
(1) Sequence detector architectural variants;
(2) Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) grounding vs ungrounded generation and quantization precision for the RCA engine;
(3) Blind human expert evaluation.

As shown in Table~\ref{tab_ablation}, removing the TCN layer degrades F1-score from $0.9521$ to $0.9214$, confirming the importance of dilated convolutions for localized temporal feature extraction. In the RCA engine, grounding generation on retrieved institutional SOPs boosts ROUGE-L from $0.7410$ (NF4 without RAG) to $0.8420$ (NF4 + RAG, a $+13.6\%$ gain in reference similarity) and BLEU-4 from $0.6850$ to $0.7815$. Evaluating retrieval quality across the 50 expert-evaluated campus incidents, where a retrieved SOP was adjudged relevant when at least two of three blind experts confirmed its applicability to the incident, FAISS vector retrieval achieves a Hit@2 of $96.0\%$ (48/50 cases successfully retrieved directly relevant institutional SOPs under $\theta_{\text{RAG}} \ge 0.75$). Furthermore, quantizing Phi-3 Mini to 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4) reduces peak allocated GPU tensor memory (measured via \texttt{torch.cuda.max\_memory\_allocated()} during generation) from $7.60\text{~GB}$ to $1.80\text{~GB}$ ($76.3\%$ compression) while maintaining high reference-text overlap ($0.8420$ vs $0.8625$ in FP16, a $97.6\%$ similarity retention) alongside high expert diagnosis correctness ($4.76/5.0$). The end-to-end RCA pipeline executes in $880.5 \pm 52.1$~ms (BGE retrieval: $4.2 \pm 0.3$~ms; prompt assembly: $1.8 \pm 0.2$~ms; LLM report synthesis: $874.5 \pm 52.0$~ms, comprising TTFT $= 42.5 \pm 2.1$~ms with prefix caching and autoregressive decoding at $5.20 \pm 0.15$~ms/token across $160 \pm 9$ JSON tokens).

\subsection{Incident Triage Latency Analysis}
To evaluate operational speedups, we conducted a baseline SOC user study where the three independent senior network security administrators each independently evaluated the 50 simulated enterprise campus incidents in randomized order without automated RCA assistance ($n = 150$ total analyst-incident trials, Fig.~\ref{fig_triage}). To eliminate carry-over bias, the manual study was completed prior to exposing experts to model-generated RCA outputs; trials were distributed across four non-consecutive sessions per analyst to prevent fatigue. Timing began upon initial alert notification and concluded upon verified diagnostic and mitigation playbook submission. Manual triage across all 150 trials averaged $15.4 \pm 2.8\text{~minutes}$ (median $14.8\text{~min}$, range $9.5$--$22.0\text{~min}$) per incident. In contrast, our automated pipeline executes TCN-Transformer detection in $0.1316\text{~ms}$ and synthesizes preliminary RAG-grounded RCA playbooks in $874.5\text{~ms}$ (total RCA pipeline $880.5\text{~ms}$), dramatically expediting the initial diagnostic draft synthesis.

\section{Real-World Campus Network Case Studies}
The following case studies illustrate two actual historical production incidents drawn from anonymized HUFLIT campus logs during the 30-day collection period:

\textbf{Case Study 1 (Active Directory Credential Stuffing Attack)}: A botnet initiated credential stuffing against campus LDAP, logging 45 authentication failures within $3.2$~s across student Wi-Fi VLANs ($S(X) = 0.942 > \tau$). The Foundation Model synthesizes:
\textbf{Severity}: \texttt{CRITICAL}; \textbf{Root Cause}: High-density Credential Stuffing targeting campus LDAP; \textbf{Technical Diagnosis}: 45 consecutive failed logins indicate dictionary spraying; \textbf{Remediation Playbook}: (1) Install OpenFlow drop rule for offending IPs; (2) Enforce 15-minute lockout; (3) Dispatch 2FA re-authentication prompts.

\textbf{Case Study 2 (Inter-VLAN Spanning Tree Protocol Loop)}: An unauthorized switch connected to a dormitory access port created a Layer 2 loop, triggering a broadcast storm saturating the inter-building uplink ($S(X) = 0.887 > \tau$). The Foundation Model produces:
\textbf{Severity}: \texttt{HIGH}; \textbf{Root Cause}: STP loop causing broadcast storm on Edge Switch 4; \textbf{Technical Diagnosis}: Surge of broadcast templates with egress drops confirms an unmanaged bridge loop; \textbf{Remediation Playbook}: (1) Enable BPDU Guard on Port 12 of Edge Switch 4; (2) Re-route traffic to backup link; (3) Issue field inspection ticket.

\section{Discussion \& Threats to Validity}
\textbf{Internal \& External Validity}: Ground-truth anomaly annotations were verified independently by senior security engineers. Multi-seed evaluation with strict chronological temporal partitioning eliminates cross-window data leakage. Evaluation across four distinct computing domains supports cross-domain applicability. Recent 2026 architectures (e.g., KRONE~\cite{ref_krone_2026}, ContraLog~\cite{ref_contralog_2026}, MaidLog~\cite{ref_maidlog_2026}, Smart Eye~\cite{ref_smarteye_2026}) adopt distinct hierarchical tokenizations or parser-free pretraining objectives; our baseline benchmarks in Table~\ref{tab_accuracy} strictly evaluate architectures that could be reproducibly retrained end-to-end under our streaming campus evaluation protocol.

\textbf{Ethics \& Data Privacy}: Production campus syslog streams were collected under authorized institutional IT oversight from HUFLIT. All telemetry underwent automated pseudonymization (IP hashing, user ID obfuscation, and credential redaction) prior to offline analysis. Participant consent and institutional ethical approval were obtained for the SOC expert user study.

\textbf{Hallucination Risk Mitigation}: Greedy decoding (\textit{do\_sample=False}) and RAG grounding on verified institutional SOPs significantly mitigate the risk of ungrounded remediation commands; no unsafe recommendations were observed across the 50 expert-rated incident playbooks.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented an LLM-Augmented AIOps Framework for real-time log anomaly detection and automated Root Cause Analysis in enterprise campus infrastructures. Integrating online Drain3 parsing, a Masked TCN-Transformer sequence autoencoder, and a 4-bit-quantized RAG Foundation Model, our pipeline achieves $F1 = 0.9521 \pm 0.004$ and ROC-AUC $= 0.9784 \pm 0.003$ on 19.28~GB campus logs, sub-millisecond detection ($0.1316$~ms CPU), and synthesizes structured RCA incident reports in $880.5$~ms (with $874.5$~ms LLM report synthesis), with 0/50 expert-rated playbooks judged unsafe. Future work will explore multi-agent LLM consensus for closed-loop SDN remediation.
