\begin{abstract}
Modern campus infrastructures emit heterogeneous logs, but real labels and leakage-resistant temporal partitions are difficult to obtain. We evaluate a masked TCN--Transformer detector on a selective HUFLIT stream of $1{,}000{,}000$ lines from five backup days. A custom similarity parser produces template IDs; heuristic HTTP-status and keyword rules provide \emph{proxy} labels rather than SOC-adjudicated ground truth. Training replaces 15\% of nominal-window tokens with a reserved mask token, and inference uses leave-one-position-out pseudo-log-likelihood. We use a stream-position $80$/$10$/$10$ split before windowing, five seeds, and $\tau=\mu_{\mathrm{val}}+3\sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$. The TCN--Transformer obtains F1 $0.0395\pm0.0163$, ROC-AUC $0.7226\pm0.0178$, and AUPRC $0.8356\pm0.0139$; removing the TCN yields F1 $0.3475\pm0.4097$ and ROC-AUC $0.7685\pm0.0351$, so the experiment does not support a TCN benefit. An audit found repeated records across dated backups (36.2\% of unique validation lines also occur in training), so these are descriptive stream results, not leakage-free generalization estimates. We additionally specify a local LLM+RAG narration interface, but the artifact contains only a prompt/schema mock and we report no RCA quality or latency result.
\keywords{AIOps \and Log Anomaly Detection \and Proxy Labels \and TCN--Transformer \and Pseudo-Log-Likelihood \and Campus Infrastructure.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Enterprise and higher-education services generate heterogeneous operational logs. Sequence models can prioritize unusual template patterns, but an unusual pattern is not by itself proof of an attack, fault, or root cause~\cite{ref_deeplog,ref_loganomaly}.

Sequence-based detectors include DeepLog~\cite{ref_deeplog}, LogAnomaly~\cite{ref_loganomaly}, LogRobust~\cite{ref_logrobust}, and SwissLog~\cite{ref_swisslog}. Published results on curated benchmarks do not remove two practical limitations:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Explainability Deficit}: Contemporary anomaly detectors typically expose only a scalar anomaly score or reconstruction error. Such numbers provide little contextual explanation of \emph{why} a sequence is anomalous, \emph{what} attack or fault vector is likely, or \emph{which} services are impacted.
    \item \textbf{Prolonged Incident Triage \& SOC Alert Fatigue}: Security Operations Center (SOC) analysts and network reliability engineers must manually query raw distributed log stores, correlate timestamps across multi-tier appliances, and draft remediation steps. In campus settings this manual triage often dominates mean-time-to-repair for multi-service incidents (credential stuffing, broadcast storms), even when the detector already raised an alert.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these operational challenges, recent 2025--2026 literature---including LLM4Log (145 studies)~\cite{ref_llm4log_survey}, KRONE (ICDE 2026)~\cite{ref_krone_2026}, MaidLog~\cite{ref_maidlog_2026}, Smart Eye (WWW 2026)~\cite{ref_smarteye_2026}, and cloud RCA recommenders~\cite{ref_llm_rca,ref_rca_survey}---already explores LLMs for log analytics and incident narration. The remaining gap for campus operators is narrower: \emph{(i)} measured evaluation on real multi-service campus telemetry (not only public HDFS/BGL), \emph{(ii)} a lightweight local detector that can gate expensive narration, and \emph{(iii)} an edge-oriented RCA \emph{design} that avoids cloud APIs. This paper addresses (i)--(ii) with measured experiments and presents (iii) as an architecture whose quantitative RCA study is still pending.

In this paper, we study a campus pipeline combining a custom similarity parser, a masked TCN--Transformer detector, and a designed local 4-bit LLM+RAG narration stage. We do \emph{not} claim a new LLM-for-logs system; the measured contribution is a campus-stream detector study with explicit proxy-label and duplicate-backup limitations.

The core contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Campus-stream evaluation protocol}: Stream-position $80$/$10$/$10$ partitioning before windowing, nominal-only masked training, $\tau=\mu+3\sigma$ calibration, five seeds, proxy-label disclosure, and quantified duplicate overlap on a $1{,}000{,}000$-line HUFLIT selective stream.
    \item \textbf{Masked TCN--Transformer detector}: Real token replacement during training, causal dilated convolutions, positional encoding, attention padding masks, and leave-one-position-out scoring.
    \item \textbf{Same-split baselines}: PCA, DeepLog, LogAnomaly-lite, a masked Transformer without TCN, and the proposed model under identical templates/splits/thresholding (\texttt{huflit\_baselines.json}).
    \item \textbf{RCA stage as design (unevaluated quantitatively)}: A planned local Phi-3/Mistral INT4 + RAG playbook interface; no ROUGE/expert metrics are reported while the harness remains mocked.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Log Parsing and Template Extraction}
Log parsing transforms free-form messages into event templates by separating constants from dynamic parameters~\cite{ref_drain3,ref_spell}. Drain uses a fixed-depth parse tree~\cite{ref_drain3}; Spell uses streaming sequence matching~\cite{ref_spell}. Our artifact does \emph{not} implement Drain or Drain3. It applies regex masking and a linear scan over equal-length templates, merging tokens when positional similarity exceeds $0.5$. We call this the \emph{custom similarity parser} and avoid attributing Drain3 properties or throughput to it.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Log Anomaly Detection}
Sequence-based anomaly detection models learn temporal transition dynamics over sequences of log templates~\cite{ref_deeplog,ref_loganomaly}. Du et al.~\cite{ref_deeplog} introduced DeepLog, employing Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks to model normal log sequences and flag unexpected subsequent templates as anomalous. Meng et al.~\cite{ref_loganomaly} proposed LogAnomaly, integrating semantic template vectors via FastText to capture semantic similarities. SwissLog~\cite{ref_swisslog} and LogRobust~\cite{ref_logrobust} further address log instability and interleaved execution threads. While deep sequence architectures achieve strong detection precision, they operate as black-box scoring engines: scalar anomaly metrics fail to explain the operational root cause, imposing heavy manual triage overhead on operators.

\subsection{Large Language Models in Log Analytics and RCA}
Recent work includes Transformer--TCN detection~\cite{ref_tranconvad}, masked and contrastive representations~\cite{ref_contralog_2026}, and LLM-assisted detection such as KRONE~\cite{ref_krone_2026}, MaidLog~\cite{ref_maidlog_2026}, CoLA~\cite{ref_cola_2025}, and Smart Eye~\cite{ref_smarteye_2026}. LLM4Log~\cite{ref_llm4log_survey} surveys this rapidly developing area. LLM-based cloud-incident recommendation has also been studied~\cite{ref_llm_rca,ref_rca_survey}. Consequently, our architecture is not novel merely for placing a detector before an LLM. We focus on the audited campus-stream experiment; the local narrator remains an unevaluated design.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{System Architecture Overview}
The studied architecture has three stages:
(i) \textbf{Stage 1 (Parsing)}: selected syslog messages are tokenized by the custom similarity parser;
(ii) \textbf{Stage 2 (Masked Sequence Anomaly Detection)}: Sliding template windows are masked, vectorized, and scored by our Masked TCN-Transformer Autoencoder;
(iii) \textbf{Stage 3 (Designed RCA Narration)}: On alerts, a local 4-bit foundation model \emph{is intended} to retrieve SOP passages and emit a structured JSON playbook; this stage is specified below but not quantitatively evaluated in the current artifact.

\subsection{Masked TCN-Transformer Denoising Sequence Model}
We formulate the detector as a masked template language model: 15\% of input tokens are replaced by a reserved mask token, representations pass through a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN)~\cite{ref_tcn} and Transformer self-attention~\cite{ref_attention}, and a linear vocabulary head predicts the original identities only at selected positions.

\subsubsection{Embedding and Dynamic Masking}
A sliding window of length $L = 20$ with stride $S = 10$ (as used in the measured HUFLIT runs) extracts template sequences $\mathbf{X} = [T_1, T_2, \ldots, T_L]$. During training, 15\% of the template tokens are randomly replaced with a reserved \texttt{[MASK]} token ($ID = |\mathcal{V}|+1$), distinguishing masked positions from sequence padding ($ID = 0$). Padding masks are applied across self-attention layers to prevent padded positions from corrupting contextual representations. Input tokens are embedded into a $d_{\text{model}}$-dimensional continuous vector space:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{E} = \text{Embedding}(\mathbf{X}) + \mathbf{P}_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_{\text{model}}}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{P}_{\text{pos}}$ denotes sinusoidal positional encodings~\cite{ref_attention}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{P}_{(pos, 2i)} = \sin\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\text{model}}}}\right), \quad \mathbf{P}_{(pos, 2i+1)} = \cos\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\text{model}}}}\right)
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) Layer}
To capture localized temporal patterns across consecutive log events, the embedded sequence $\mathbf{E}$ passes through a stack of 1D dilated causal convolutional layers~\cite{ref_tcn}. For an input sequence $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^L$ and a convolutional filter $f: \{0, \ldots, k-1\} \to \mathbb{R}$, the dilated convolution operation with dilation factor $d$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{y}(t) = (\mathbf{x} *_d f)(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} f(i) \cdot \mathbf{x}(t - d \cdot i)
\end{equation}
We configure dilation factors $d \in \{1, 2\}$, enforcing causal convolutions for localized temporal extraction without future feature leakage. Residual connections and layer normalization ensure stable gradient propagation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{H}_{\text{TCN}} = \text{LayerNorm}(\text{Proj}(\mathbf{E}) + \text{GELU}(\text{CausalTCN}(\mathbf{E})))
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Transformer Multi-Head Self-Attention Encoder}
The output $\mathbf{H}_{\text{TCN}}$ is then fed into a Transformer Encoder block to model long-range inter-template correlations. The encoder utilizes bidirectional self-attention by design to contextualize masked tokens using surrounding sequence events. For queries $\mathbf{Q}$, keys $\mathbf{K}$, and values $\mathbf{V}$:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Attention}(\mathbf{Q}, \mathbf{K}, \mathbf{V}) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{K}^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) \mathbf{V}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \text{MHA}(\mathbf{H}) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \ldots, \text{head}_h) \mathbf{W}^O
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \text{head}_i = \text{Attention}(\mathbf{H} \mathbf{W}_i^Q, \mathbf{H} \mathbf{W}_i^K, \mathbf{H} \mathbf{W}_i^V)
\end{equation}
The Transformer block uses pre-layer normalization, residual connections, and a two-layer feed-forward network with GELU activation:
\begin{equation}
    \text{FFN}(\mathbf{Z}) = \mathrm{GELU}(\mathbf{Z} \mathbf{W}_1 + \mathbf{b}_1) \mathbf{W}_2 + \mathbf{b}_2
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{H} + \text{MHA}(\text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{H})), \quad
    \mathbf{H}_{\text{out}} = \mathbf{Z} + \text{FFN}(\text{LayerNorm}(\mathbf{Z})).
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Masked Reconstruction Objective and Anomaly Scoring}
A linear classification head projects $\mathbf{H}_{\text{out}}$ back to the template vocabulary space $\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{V}|}$, serving as the reconstruction decoder. The model is trained to predict original template identities at masked positions via Cross-Entropy loss:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{MLM}} = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{M}} \log P(T_i = t_i \mid \mathbf{X}_{\backslash \mathcal{M}})
\end{equation}
During inference, we compute pseudo-log-likelihood by making one forward pass per valid position (batched over sequences), masking only that position and excluding padding ($ID=0$):
\begin{equation}
    S(\mathbf{X}) = \frac{1}{L_{\text{valid}}} \sum_{t=1}^{L_{\text{valid}}} -\log P(T_t = t_t \mid \mathbf{X}_{\backslash \{t\}})
\end{equation}
A validation-calibrated threshold $\tau$ flags anomalous sequences:
\begin{equation}
    \tau = \mu_{\text{val}} + 3 \cdot \sigma_{\text{val}}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{\text{val}}$ and $\sigma_{\text{val}}$ are the mean and standard deviation of reconstruction loss over nominal validation sequences.

\subsection{RAG-Grounded Foundation Model RCA Narration Engine (Design)}
When $S(X)>\tau$, the intended Stage~3 activates a local RAG+LLM narrator. This subsection specifies the design; it is not backed by measured LLM outputs in the current repository.

\textbf{RAG Knowledge Base \& Vector Retrieval (design)}: The intended path would index institutional runbooks with a dense retriever and FAISS, then prompt a local quantized model for JSON playbooks. We report no SOP count, retrieval metric, RCA quality, or LLM latency: \texttt{rca\_narration.py} only builds a prompt and returns null-valued schema fields marked \texttt{DESIGN\_ONLY\_NOT\_MODEL\_OUTPUT}.

\textbf{Prompt schema (design only)}: The planned prompt would integrate anomalous templates, infrastructure metadata, and retrieved SOP snippets, requesting JSON fields \texttt{severity}, \texttt{root\_cause\_summary}, \texttt{technical\_diagnosis}, and \texttt{remediation\_playbook}. Claims about measured NF4/AWQ VRAM (e.g., $7.60\rightarrow1.80$~GB) are removed until a non-mock LLM run writes those readings.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\subsection{Experimental Setup and Benchmark Datasets}
We conduct a measured empirical evaluation on HUFLIT campus logs:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{HUFLIT Campus Logs (Measured Stream)}: The persisted arrays contain $1{,}000{,}000$ non-empty lines and $368{,}940{,}230$ streamed text bytes, capped at $200$k lines for each of five backup days and $40$k per member. Source shares by line are careerhub $48.60\%$, aca $26.09\%$, courses $24.07\%$, portal $0.31\%$, and one eligible FortiGate log $0.94\%$; library/web sources contribute zero lines. FortiGate CSV files and members larger than $500$~MiB were excluded. Proxy labels use HTTP $4$xx/$5$xx, deny/block fields, and keyword rules; the line proxy-anomaly rate is $7.96\%$.
    \item \textbf{Public Benchmarks (Deferred)}: Full re-runs of BGL, HDFS, and UNSW-NB15 under the same hardened harness are left to a follow-up revision so that campus numbers remain strictly measured rather than mixed with prior placeholder tables.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Partitioning and overlap}: Records are ordered by backup day, ZIP name, member name, and in-member position, then split $80\%$/$10\%$/$10\%$ before sliding windows ($L=20$, step $10$). This is a \emph{stream-position split}, not an event-time split. A raw-line hash audit found $34{,}770$ validation-unique lines also in training ($36.235\%$ of unique validation lines); validation/test overlap is $8{,}429$ lines ($11.382\%$ of unique test lines). Exact sequence overlap is smaller but nonzero: 71 train/validation and 112 validation/test unique sequences. Thus we make no leakage-free claim. Training uses 15\% masked token modeling on nominal windows; $\tau=\mu_{\mathrm{val}}+3\sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$ is calibrated on nominal validation scores. Seeds are $42,101,202,303,404$.

\textbf{Hardware \& Software Setup}: Corrected masked-model runs used CPU only with two PyTorch intra-op threads and one inter-op thread (PyTorch $2.8.0$+cpu, Windows 11, Python $3.13$). The GPU was not used. Timing is wall clock and includes model forward/scoring overhead.

\enlargethispage{5.5\baselineskip}
\subsection{Inference Latency}
Correct leave-one-position-out scoring requires 20 forward passes per length-20 window. On the CPU run, the proposed model averages $52.917\pm4.990$~ms at batch 1 and $5.396\pm0.373$~ms per sequence at batch 512. The no-TCN masked model averages $2.044\pm0.510$~ms at batch 1. Retained PCA/LSTM rows have only legacy batched timing and are marked NR rather than relabeled. LLM playbook latency is not claimed.

\section{Discussion \& Threats to Validity}
\textbf{Internal \& External Validity}: Proxy labels, backup duplication, stream-order rather than event-time partitioning, and test anomaly prevalence ($80.5\%$) limit causal and operational interpretation. Gold SOC labels, deduplication before splitting, event-time ordering, and a held-out collection period are required before deployment claims. The custom parser is also unevaluated against parsing ground truth.

\textbf{Ethics \& Data Privacy}: Production campus telemetry is kept private. Any future release requires authorization, IP/credential redaction, retention review, and assessment of whether template text can reveal personal or security-sensitive information.

\textbf{Hallucination Risk}: Until measured LLM runs exist, remediation playbooks must not be treated as validated operator guidance.

\section{Conclusion}
We evaluated a masked TCN--Transformer on a $1{,}000{,}000$-line selective campus stream and documented a local RCA interface as design only. The present proxy labels, backup duplication, and stream-position split preclude leakage-free or deployment-level claims. Future work must deduplicate before an event-time split, obtain SOC-adjudicated labels, test on a later collection period, and evaluate a non-mock narrator with retrieved evidence.
