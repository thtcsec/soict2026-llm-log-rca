\begin{abstract}
Campus log anomaly studies often treat archive order as time and window OR-aggregation as a label. We measure how those choices distort conclusions on a selective HUFLIT stream ($1{,}000{,}000$ deduplicated lines; custom similarity parser; HTTP/keyword \emph{proxy} labels). On a leaky stream-position split, test-window prevalence reaches $80.5\%$ because a window is positive if any of $L{=}20$ lines is positive; an always-anomaly classifier then obtains F1$\approx0.892$, while a masked TCN--Transformer under $\tau=\mu{+}3\sigma$ scores only $0.0395\pm0.0163$ F1 despite ROC-AUC $0.7226\pm0.0178$ (AUPRC lift over prevalence $\approx{+}0.031$). After global line-hash deduplication, source/member-bounded windows, and a day-held-out split (train 15--17~Jun, val 18~Jun, test 19~Jun 2026), test prevalence falls to $56.4\%$ (always-anomaly F1 baseline $0.721$), yet masked models collapse to near-chance ranking (TCN--Transformer ROC-AUC $0.5315\pm0.112$; no-TCN $0.4933\pm0.009$), while PCA retains a modest AUPRC lift ($+0.098$). Threshold sweeps show $\mu{+}3\sigma$ suppresses recall; looser rules inflate F1 toward the high-prevalence trivial baseline. The TCN adds latency (batch-1 $51.1$ vs.\ $18.5$~ms) without a consistent accuracy benefit. Local LLM RCA is left to future work.
\keywords{AIOps \and Log Anomaly Detection \and Proxy Labels \and Experimental Validity \and TCN--Transformer \and Campus Infrastructure.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Campus IT services emit heterogeneous operational logs. Sequence detectors can score unusual template patterns~\cite{ref_deeplog,ref_loganomaly,ref_logrobust,ref_swisslog}, but archive packaging, duplicate backups, and heuristic labels can invent ``temporal'' structure that models then appear to learn.

This paper treats those validity threats as first-class experimental objects. We study a masked TCN--Transformer and a no-TCN ablated Transformer on HUFLIT selective extracts, with two protocols: (A)~a leaky stream-position split that mirrors a common but flawed archive ordering; (B)~a deduplicated, source-bounded, day-held-out split. We add trivial baselines (always-anomaly; no-skill AUPRC $=$ prevalence), report AUPRC lift over prevalence, and sweep validation thresholds. We do \emph{not} claim an LLM root-cause system; local narration is deferred.

The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Validity protocol}: quantify how OR window labels and archive-order splits inflate prevalence and inflate apparent F1 relative to an always-anomaly trivial baseline; retain dual-split evaluation artifacts (\texttt{huflit\_baselines.json}, \texttt{huflit\_v2\_*}).
    \item \textbf{Dedup + day split}: global SHA-1 line dedup, windows that never cross (day, source, member), train/val/test by backup day.
    \item \textbf{Threshold-aware reading}: $\mu{+}k\sigma$ and percentile sweeps plus a validation-oracle diagnostic, separating ranking from calibration.
    \item \textbf{Negative TCN evidence}: on both protocols the TCN does not consistently beat a masked Transformer without TCN, while increasing leave-one-out scoring latency.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Log Parsing and Template Extraction}
Log parsing transforms free-form messages into event templates by separating constants from dynamic parameters~\cite{ref_drain3,ref_spell}. Drain uses a fixed-depth parse tree~\cite{ref_drain3}; Spell uses streaming sequence matching~\cite{ref_spell}. Our artifact does \emph{not} implement Drain or Drain3. It applies regex masking and a linear scan over equal-length templates, merging tokens when positional similarity exceeds $0.5$. We call this the \emph{custom similarity parser} and avoid attributing Drain3 properties or throughput to it.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Log Anomaly Detection}
Sequence-based anomaly detection models learn temporal transition dynamics over sequences of log templates~\cite{ref_deeplog,ref_loganomaly,ref_pca}. Du et al.~\cite{ref_deeplog} introduced DeepLog (LSTM next-token prediction). Meng et al.~\cite{ref_loganomaly} proposed LogAnomaly with semantic template vectors. SwissLog~\cite{ref_swisslog} and LogRobust~\cite{ref_logrobust} address instability and interleaved threads. Classical reconstruction baselines such as PCA on count vectors remain competitive on some structured logs~\cite{ref_pca}. Recent Transformer--TCN hybrids~\cite{ref_tranconvad}, multi-scale semantic models~\cite{ref_logmuse_2026}, and contrastive masked objectives~\cite{ref_contralog_2026} continue this line. Public corpora such as Loghub~\cite{ref_loghub} remain the standard sanity setting; we defer a full HDFS/BGL re-run under the present harness to future work.

\subsection{LLMs for Log Analytics}
LLM-assisted detectors include KRONE~\cite{ref_krone_2026}, MaidLog~\cite{ref_maidlog_2026}, CoLA~\cite{ref_cola_2025}, and Smart Eye~\cite{ref_smarteye_2026}. LLM4Log~\cite{ref_llm4log_survey} surveys the area; cloud RCA recommenders are studied in~\cite{ref_llm_rca,ref_rca_survey}. Our focus is experimental validity on campus archives rather than a new LLM narrator.

\section{Methodology}
\subsection{System Architecture Overview}
The measured system has two stages:
(i) \textbf{Parsing}: selected syslog messages are tokenized by the custom similarity parser;
(ii) \textbf{Masked sequence scoring}: sliding template windows are corrupted by true token replacement, encoded by an optional causal TCN plus Transformer, and scored by leave-one-position-out pseudo-log-likelihood (PLL).
Local LLM+RAG narration is \emph{out of scope} (Future Work).

\subsection{Masked TCN-Transformer Denoising Sequence Model}
We formulate the detector as a masked template language model: 15\% of input tokens are replaced by a reserved mask token, representations pass through a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN)~\cite{ref_tcn} and Transformer self-attention~\cite{ref_attention}, and a linear vocabulary head predicts the original identities only at selected positions.

\subsubsection{Embedding and Dynamic Masking}
A sliding window of length $L = 20$ with stride $S = 10$ extracts template sequences $\mathbf{X} = [T_1, T_2, \ldots, T_L]$. During training, 15\% of tokens are replaced with \texttt{[MASK]} ($ID = |\mathcal{V}|+1$), distinct from padding ($ID = 0$). Padding masks are applied in self-attention. Embeddings use sinusoidal positional encodings~\cite{ref_attention}:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{E} = \text{Embedding}(\mathbf{X}) + \mathbf{P}_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times d_{\text{model}}}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) Layer}
Dilated causal convolutions~\cite{ref_tcn} with $d \in \{1, 2\}$ extract local patterns without future leakage:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{y}(t) = (\mathbf{x} *_d f)(t) = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} f(i) \cdot \mathbf{x}(t - d \cdot i)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{H}_{\text{TCN}} = \text{LayerNorm}(\text{Proj}(\mathbf{E}) + \text{GELU}(\text{CausalTCN}(\mathbf{E})))
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Transformer Encoder and Scoring}
A single Transformer encoder layer (pre-LN, multi-head attention, GELU FFN) contextualizes the sequence. Training minimizes masked cross-entropy. Inference uses leave-one-position-out PLL:
\begin{equation}
    S(\mathbf{X}) = \frac{1}{L_{\text{valid}}} \sum_{t=1}^{L_{\text{valid}}} -\log P(T_t = t_t \mid \mathbf{X}_{\backslash \{t\}})
\end{equation}
The primary threshold is $\tau=\mu_{\mathrm{val}}+3\sigma_{\mathrm{val}}$ on nominal validation scores; we also report $k\in\{1,2,3\}$, percentiles $\{95,97.5,99\}$, and a validation-oracle F1 threshold as a diagnostic only.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
\subsection{Corpus and Window Labels}
We stream selective Thang6 day zips from a local campus extract (\path{D:/huflit-campus-logs}), skipping \texttt{fortigate.csv} and members ${>}500$~MiB. Proxy line labels use HTTP $4$xx/$5$xx, deny/block fields, and keywords---not SOC adjudication. A window is positive iff \emph{any} of its $L$ lines is positive (OR aggregation). Under independence, $1-(1-p)^{L}$ with $p{=}0.0796$ predicts prevalence $\approx0.81$, matching Protocol~A's $80.5\%$ test rate.

\subsection{Protocol A (leaky archive split)}
Records are ordered by backup day $\rightarrow$ ZIP $\rightarrow$ member $\rightarrow$ in-member position, then split $80$/$10$/$10$ before windowing. This is \emph{not} event time: windows may cross file/service boundaries. A raw-line hash audit of this split found $36.235\%$ of unique validation lines also in training (and $11.382\%$ validation/test line overlap). Results: \texttt{huflit\_baselines.json}.

\subsection{Protocol B (dedup + source-bounded + day split)}
We keep the first SHA-1 of each \emph{raw line} ($262{,}194$ duplicates skipped while filling $1$M unique lines), emit windows only inside one (day, source, member) group, and assign days $15$--$17$~Jun$\rightarrow$train, $18$~Jun$\rightarrow$val, $19$~Jun$\rightarrow$test. Day folders are backup collection periods, not verified per-event timestamps. Sources: aca/careerhub/courses. Test-window prevalence is $56.36\%$ (always-anomaly F1 baseline $0.721$).

Raw-line dedup does \emph{not} imply unique template windows: different raw strings can map to the same template-ID sequence, and similar traffic on different days can yield identical windows. We therefore report \textbf{exact template-ID window sequence overlap} (SHA-1 of the integer window vector): $505$ train/val and $110$ train/test shared windows (\texttt{huflit\_v2\_split\_meta.json}).

\subsection{Results}
On Protocol~A, DeepLog leads ROC-AUC ($0.781$); the TCN is \emph{worse} than no-TCN on mean F1/AUC and much slower at batch-1 PLL. The always-anomaly F1 baseline ($0.892$) exceeds every learned F1 at $\mu{+}3\sigma$; AUPRC must be read against prevalence $0.805$.

On Protocol~B, masked models lose day-to-day ranking (AUC$\approx0.49$--$0.53$). PCA keeps a small positive AUPRC lift ($+0.098$). Table~\ref{tab_thr}: $\mu{+}3\sigma$ starves recall; $\mu{+}1\sigma$ F1 ($0.696$) approaches the always-anomaly baseline ($0.721$) without improving AUC---loose thresholds harvest prevalence, not representation quality. Oracle-val F1 ($0.741$) is only marginally above the trivial baseline. TCN remains slower ($51$ vs.\ $19$~ms) without consistent accuracy gains.

Due to privacy restrictions, raw campus telemetry cannot be redistributed. We retain code, configuration, aggregate split statistics, and derived evaluation JSON artifacts in the project repository for co-author reproduction; a public mirror is contingent on institutional approval.

\section{Discussion \& Threats to Validity}
\textbf{What sequence means}: Protocol~A orders archive members, not wall-clock events. Protocol~B removes cross-member windows and duplicate raw lines but still uses backup-day folders rather than verified timestamps.

\textbf{Window OR labels}: OR aggregation manufactures high prevalence and makes the always-anomaly F1 baseline a necessary reference (not an upper bound: a perfect detector still has F1$=1$). Ranking metrics and lift-over-prevalence must accompany thresholded F1.

\textbf{Proxy labels and label--feature coupling}: HTTP status tokens, deny/block fields, and keyword heuristics both \emph{define} proxy labels and often remain visible in template text. Models may therefore learn the labeling rule rather than operational anomalies. Gold SOC labels and keyword-ablated inputs are required before operational claims.

\textbf{Ethics}: Campus telemetry stays private; any future release needs redaction and authorization.

\section{Conclusion}
We showed that archive-order splits and OR window labels can inflate prevalence and make thresholded F1 look stronger than ranking warrants on HUFLIT campus logs. After deduplication, source-bounded windows, and day holdout, masked TCN/Transformer ranking collapses while PCA retains a modest AUPRC lift; TCN adds latency without a reliable benefit. Future work: event timestamps, SOC labels, Loghub~\cite{ref_loghub} sanity checks under the same harness, keyword-ablated inputs, and---separately---measured local LLM narration.
